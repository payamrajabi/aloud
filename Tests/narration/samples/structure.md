# Pour-Over Coffee at Home

Pour-over is the simplest way to make a clean, bright cup. It takes about four minutes, and most of that is waiting. You don't need much, but you do need to slow down.

## What You Need

Gather these before you start:

- A kettle, ideally one with a **thin spout**
- A paper filter and a dripper
- A scale that reads in grams
- Fresh beans, ground *medium-fine*

If you only buy one thing, make it the scale. Guessing by eye is the most common reason a cup tastes different from one day to the next.

## The Method

### Rinse and Weigh

Rinse the filter with hot water. This warms the dripper and washes away the papery taste. Pour that water out, then weigh twenty grams of coffee into the filter and give it a gentle shake to level the bed.

### Brew

1. Heat the water until it's just off the boil.
2. Pour about forty grams, enough to wet all the grounds.
3. Wait <u>thirty seconds</u> while the coffee blooms.
4. Pour the rest in slow circles until the scale reads three hundred and twenty grams.

The whole pour should take around three and a half minutes. If it runs much longer, the cup turns bitter; much shorter, and it tastes thin.

#### A Note on Grind Size

If the water drains too fast, grind finer. If it stalls, grind ~~much~~ a little coarser. Change one thing at a time, and taste the difference before you change another.

> Good coffee is mostly patience. The rest is paying attention.

## Ratios

Most people settle somewhere in this range:

| Strength | Coffee | Water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | 18 grams | 320 grams |
| Classic | 20 grams | 320 grams |
| Strong | 22 grams | 320 grams |

## For the Curious

If you like to keep notes, a tiny script helps. Run `brew-log add` after each cup:

```sh
brew-log add --dose 20 --water 320 --time 3:45
```

Over a few weeks the notes show you which beans you reach for and which ratio you keep coming back to. There is a longer walkthrough in [the full brewing guide](https://example.com/pour-over).
